# HD models

How every model's HD version is built: the HD model kit for Builder models, the city, castles, the town hall and actors, and the hand-built heroes, bosses and creatures. Read with [HD_WORKFLOW.md](HD_WORKFLOW.md) (the quality bar, checking and measuring) before any HD work.

## HD model kit (every model's HD version)

**Before any HD work, on any tier, read [HD_WORKFLOW.md](HD_WORKFLOW.md):** the quality bar (what each tier's HD must add, the reference models to compare against, the order to work in), how to check a model in the Browser pane (dev handles, playing actions on a fixed clock, stand-ins for what the guest realm lacks, proving Normal unchanged), how to measure draws and GPU time, and the gotchas met so far. Add to it when you learn a new trick or trap.

Every Builder model (map NPCs, roving bands, resource nodes, landmarks, gates, realms) has **one part list and two renderings**: Normal draws it exactly as before; HD (graphics feature `models`, `InstancedParts kit`, the Codex's ✨ HD) draws the same parts through the kit. Legendary and mythic heroes are hand-built instead (next section).

- **What the kit does by itself:** each shape's HD twin (`getShapeGeometry(shape, true)`: bevelled box, soft-rimmed cylinder, rounder sphere and cone), smooth shading, soft AO (model ground via `Part.base`, undersides, each part's foot), and the part's material. Smoke stays as in Normal.
- **Part options for HD** (`Builder.add`, ignored in Normal):
  - `mat: Surface`: what it is made of (`fur`, `hide`, `skin`, `cloth`, `leather`, `wood`, `bone`, `stone`, `dull` / `iron` / `steel` / `gold` metal, `gem`, `lava`, `gel` (wet slime: glossy, lit through at the rim, it wobbles), `leaf` (foliage and crops: clumps, darker inside, each part its own shade), `masonry` (dressed stone courses on its sides, flagstones on top: walls, roads, plinths), `tiles` (overlapping roof tiles or slates down any face turned up: prisms, pyramids, cones, domes); `SURFACES` in `parts.ts` holds roughness and metalness). Its surface and bump are drawn in the part's own space, so they stay on a walking creature, and they fade by screen size.
  - `hd: true`: HD-only detail (buckles, straps, rivets, fur tufts, coat tails). `hd: false`: Normal-only, for a part the HD version replaces with a different size or shape (the wolf's chest block, its stick tail). Pairs of these never meet: `check:models` checks Normal and HD separately.
  - `hdShape`: the same box drawn as another shape in HD (`limb`, `taper`, `pod`, `snout`, `egg`, `drop`, `horn`, `capsule`, `boulder`, …).
  - `sync: n`: HD detail riding a swaying Normal part (ears on a wheat clump, grapes on a vine) takes the same `sync` number and `idle` kind, so it moves exactly with the first part of its group (its anchor, phase, tempo and size) instead of drifting apart: `animateParts` gives every part its own seeded tempo otherwise. Give the Normal part the number first (it keeps its own motion).
  - `pose: { at, rx, ry, rz }`: a relaxed rest pose in HD, turned about the joint at local `at` (an arm about its shoulder, a head about its neck). Seed it per creature (`restRng` in `spawnModels.ts`), never mirror left and right, and give a held item the same pose as its arm.
- **Kits first.** `figure` and `beast` already tag their parts (materials, HD shapes, HD-only belt, buckle, pouch, boot cuffs, shoulders, closed fists with a thumb, paws, tufts, rest poses), so a creature built from them gets an HD version for free; a model adds its own HD-only detail on top.
- **Shared HD pieces** live in `world/kitParts.ts`: `along` (a part laid from one point to another, at any angle), `arc` (parts round an arc: jaws, rings, loops), `tail` (a tail of overlapping capsule segments that curls at rest; each segment trails from the same root a little later than the last, so it bends and whips and its wag ripples down it: never one stiff stick), `bone` (a shaft with knobbed ends), `skull`, `lanternFrame`, `wheel` (a spoked wheel in any plane; drawn over part of a turn it is broken) and `coins` (stacks and strays spilled round a point, from the caller's own rng). Give every tail in an HD version a `tail(...)` and keep the Normal stick `hd: false`.
- **Shields, lanterns, tails with stingers.** A shield straps to the left forearm (`roundShield`), a lantern's light shows through its frame (`lanternFrame`), and a stinger or tuft rides a tail's end (`tail` returns its tip, direction and last trail).
- **Faces.** A `figure` gets its HD face from the kit (`faceAt` puts it on any other head; `allMat` turns a whole figure to one material, e.g. a stone statue) (`face` in `spawnModels.ts`: eyeballs under lids, brow, nose, mouth on a chin, ears; `face: "beast"` for a model that adds its own snout, `"skull"` for the dead; `nose: false` / `ears: false` when the model adds its own). A hat, mask or hood must cover what it hides (`nose: false` under a mask). A beast's muzzle grows out of its head, never hangs in front of it.
- **Big surfaces.** A big rock, mound or snowbank takes `hide` (stone's crack cells read as crazy paving that large), a painted wall, stucco or a big pale dressed-stone tower `plain` (dark stone walls may keep `stone`). A glowing bar that spins (a lighthouse's sweep) reads as a bar: in HD give it a shape of light, e.g. cones spreading from the lamp riding its turn (`sync`).
- **Small props count.** Bones, skulls, signs, stones round a fire: in HD they get their real shape and material, not a primitive standing in for them.
- **Held things sit in the hand.** In HD a weapon or tool is gripped: its grip passes through the fist (`fist(side)` in `spawnModels.ts`), never floating in front of it. Lay grips, shafts, blades and clubs with `along`, and use the HD weapon helpers (`sword`, `spear`, `club`; the `blade` shape for sword blades and spear points), the Normal weapon marked `hd: false`. A held thing takes `hand(side)`, so it swings and rests with the arm.
- **By tier:** common gets materials and well-made shapes only, matte, with `dull` metal and no shine; uncommon adds its accent colour in a real material and a few finer props; rare adds real metal (`iron` / `steel`) fittings and trim; epic gets the most HD-only detail and secondary motion, and its glow (`lava`, glowing parts). Never borrow a higher tier's glow or metal to make a lower one look better.
- **Flat parts laid with `along`.** `along` turns a part's +y onto the line, but which of `w` / `d` ends up flat depends on the line's direction (a leaf or plank laid mostly sideways stands on edge). For flat things that lie across (leaflets, rugs, ley lines) give the part its own `rz` / `ry`, or use a round shape (`capsule`).
- **Staged and broken models.** Rising, crumbling and a broken gate are generated from the part list (`landmarkStages.ts`): they seed each Normal part by its place among the Normal parts and HD-only parts apart (`partSeeds`), and fallen HD detail just disappears (so does HD detail riding an orbit, a bird's wings or a lantern's cage, when the crumbling drops orbiting parts), so HD never changes a Normal stage. A staged model (a realm's stage builds on the one before) takes one HD rng per build, the same for every stage, so a stage's part list still starts with the whole of the one before.
- **Normal must not change.** HD-only parts don't shift Normal's idle seeds (`animateParts` seeds Normal parts by their place among Normal parts) and never push a Normal part in `separateCoplanar`. After editing a model, compare its Normal part list with the previous build when in doubt.
- **The city** is a tagged scene: the improvements, the forest ring and every plot are built on builders with `kit` set (`Part.kit`) and the city draws `InstancedParts kit="tagged"`; anything new built on an untagged builder stays Normal in HD. Tag every Normal part with `mat` (or set `Builder.mat` on a child builder for a whole model): `masonry` for dressed stone and paving, `tiles` for tile and slate roofs, `plain` for plaster and painted walls, `wood`, `fur` for thatch and straw, `hide` for orc roofs and earth, `leaf` for leaf roofs, crops and turf, metals by tier (`dull` on common). Every new kit shape kind is a draw in the city. Long boxes become plain `slab`s on their own; thin trim that repeats by the hundred over a city (window frames and leading, balusters, arrow slits, merlon caps, portcullis bars) takes `hdShape: "slab"` too (12 triangles instead of a bevelled box's 108; the Archon's 50 Celestial plots are where this adds up). `sync` is resolved only in map models: in the city, HD detail that rides a moving part (a hem or crest on a fluttering banner) goes through `ride(b, host, …)` in `blueprints.ts`, which gives it the host part's own idle (anchor, phase and tempo), so it moves exactly with it at any height (a part's tempo comes from its phase, so matching `idle`, `pivot` and `phase` by hand only works at the same foot height). The `detail` list (small props hidden when zoomed out) is drawn `kit="tagged"` as well, so an HD-only part riding a detail prop takes `detail: true` with it. Zoomed in, the city's heaviest kit batches draw only their chunks of the ground in view (`chunk`, RENDERING.md *Vertices and what is off screen*), so a city's HD cost up close follows what is on screen; zoomed out, it is every vertex of every plot, so keep counting them.
- **District structures** (`city/<civ>Structures.ts`) add their HD detail through the structure kit (`structureKit.ts`): `win` (frame, sill, glazing bars or battens; pass a `Trim` for stone, silver or obsidian frames), `door` (a Normal leaf exactly as before, plus an HD frame, and bands with a ring pull when given a metal), `chimney`, `vane`, `circling`, `bale`, `lampPost`, `pennant`. `door()` and the other helpers add a Normal part too, so never call them for HD-only detail; use `hd(mat)` options instead. An HD part that moves takes its phase from `seedPhase` (its place), never `ctx.rng`, and a part riding a Normal one copies its `idle`, `pivot` and `phase`.
- **World-map castles** are their own pipeline too (`WorldCastles`, parts merged per civ): tag each Normal part with its HD `surf` and `courses` (`masonry` on walls and towers, `tiles` on roofs), mark parts its HD version replaces `hd: false` (or `hdMaxStage` when only a higher stage's HD version replaces it), and build HD-only detail in `castleHd.ts` with `hdParts({ minStage, maxStage, anim })` and the Builder kit (cloth flutters from its `pivot`, `hang` for a banner on a wall, smoke rises, a glowing puff rises as a spark, `idle: spin` turns about the upright axis through its `pivot` (`turn` speed and `bob`; every part of one call turns together), `idle: pulse` glows and fades). Moving detail rides these GPU waves inside the batches the castle already has, never a new `anim` key. HD parts from `minStage: 4` are drawn only on Wonders (their own batches), so a Wonder's layer costs nothing on the other castles. In HD each batch is drawn once per stage the civ's castles stand at, with only that stage's parts (a draw more per stage in use, about half the vertices), so a castle runs only its own stage's parts; still keep detail light (`twinOf` picks plain shapes for small parts) and give a detail a higher stage replaces a `maxStage`. Reuse the batch keys a civ already has (opacity, sidedness, shadow, the exact `anim` object): a new key is a draw per civ on the map. A vertex shader takes at most 16 attributes: pack new per-vertex values into the existing four-wide ones. Fetching them is most of an HD castle's cost, so `packHd` stores normals, colours, emissive and stages in smaller types (a frame differs by at most one step of 255); the part's own space and size, the motion pivots, the seed and positions stay full floats, since half floats there move pattern edges and moving detail. A new value goes in the smallest type that keeps frames within that step: check it with a `readPixels` diff.
- **The city's town hall** (`TownHall3D`, hand-written JSX) takes the kit through `MergeStatic hd`: each merged mesh carries `aLocal` / `aSize` / `aPbr` from its own geometry and its material's `userData.kit` (`KitTag`: `surf`, `course` 1 flat masonry, 2 round, 3 tiles, `flat` for facets), and buckets split only on emissive, sidedness, flat shading and shadows, so HD costs vertices, not draws (each bucket is indexed: a corner its faces share runs the shader once). In HD the hall's `Box` / `Cyl` / `Cone` take bevelled twins and `Mat` reads the material from the civ's palette (`kitOf`: walls courses, roof tiles, trim plain, door wood, banner cloth, metal gold or iron); give a part `surf` when its colour says the wrong thing (bark, leaves, bone, a painted crest). HD-only detail goes in `HallDetail`, by stage on the ladder (the Wonder's in `WonderDetail`, `TowerGilt`, `CrownHd` and the signatures; its HD prestige in `PrestigeHd`); keep small repeated detail light (octahedra, not balls: a merged hall's vertices are its HD cost); a new glow there should reuse an emissive the hall already has (`st.glow` at 1.3), or it is a new draw. `BossModel` uses `MergeStatic` without `hd`, unchanged.
- **Actors (townsfolk, animals, soldiers, sails, gears)** are their own pipeline: Normal keeps `actorTemplates.ts` untouched; HD models live in `city/actorHd.ts` as Builder part lists in the actor's frame (feet at the origin, facing +z, the Normal template's size and silhouette), built on a walker so parts take roles (`leg`, `arm` and `hand(side)` for what a fist holds, `head`, `torso`, `trail`, `flap`). Colours are fixed or the actor's slots (`@slot`, `@slot*f`, via `S()` / `tone()`, never `shade()` on a slot). `ActorsHd.tsx` moves the parts on the GPU from each agent's `gaitOf` (stride, walking, work / fight / cheer), one mesh per shape for every kind together (a new kind adds no draw unless it brings a new shape kind); a carried light is built on its bearer's body so it rides the same hand. Keep actor parts few and small (they take coarse twins under 0.1 across), and check them in Codex → Creatures → Folk & Beasts; `check:models` covers their HD models.
- **Models drawn one each.** The hand-tuned sights (`HAND_TUNED_NPCS`) draw a model per copy, so each shape kind (and light twin) their HD version adds is a draw per copy: keep them to the kinds they already use plus one or two.
- **Cost:** everything stays instanced (draw calls grow only with new shape kinds a model uses), motion stays on the GPU, and a new surface goes in the kit's shader as one more branch, gated behind `mid` like the others. Vertices are the HD cost on walkers (each one works out the walk), so every kit shape is indexed (`indexExact`; a new crafted shape is too, through `normalise`): small, thin or flat parts (middle size under 0.12, or least under 0.05) get their shape's lighter twin by themselves (`hdLo`), so don't build big things out of many tiny round parts, and keep an HD model's own extra parts to what reads. An HD model's own randomness takes its own seeded rng (`mulberry32`, `restRng`), never the model's `rng`, or Normal changes.

## High-graphics (HD) Codex showcases

A few top-tier models also have a hand-built **HD** version: `world/HighGraphicsSingularity.tsx` (Xal'Vorath, with action buttons: the Codex, and its cast on the login flyover, where the map's HD reflections replace its studio ones and its full-screen effects show only while it casts) and `world/HighGraphicsEmpyrean.tsx` (the Archon's castle: the Codex, the HD map through `HeroMount` and the Archon's HD town hall through `TownHall3D`). The legendary landmarks, the Nethergarde Core (standing and risen) and the legendary sites (the Dragonstone Core, the King's Motherlode, the Relic Hoard) are **batched heroes**: hand-built in `world/heroLandmarks.ts` / `world/heroCore.ts` / `world/heroSites.ts` on the hero kit (`world/heroKit.ts`, `Kit` with `motion` and `indexed`), registered in `BATCHED_HEROES` (`world/heroes.ts`) and drawn by `HeroBatch` (the Codex's `hero_hd` preview, and on the map through `HeroMount`) in the four shared hero materials (`world/heroMaterials.ts`: matte, metal, unlit glow, cloth; builders name finer buckets like `rock`, `leaf`, `iron`, `gloss`, folded in by `BUCKET_OF` as surface kinds) with the legendary `<HeroAura>`. There is only ever one of each, so they may spend more than a kit model, but they follow these rules (a hero shown on the map or in a city also keeps to that view's draw budget and brings no lights).
- **A new one-of-a-kind sight without CPU motion is a batched hero**: a builder in the kit, parts in the shared materials (a new look is a surface kind in `heroMaterials.ts`, not a new material: each material is a draw on the map), motion through `Kit.move` (GPU), effects as sparks and its aura entry. Keep it light in vertices (the map is vertex-bound on integrated GPUs: round parts with few segments, no tiny round parts by the hundred). A hero that needs CPU motion (actions, chains laid out every frame) stays a component and goes into `HeroMount` beside the Empyrean.
- **Hero models load lazily**: they ship in their own chunk, fetched only when HD is on through `world/hdModels.ts` (`useHdModels` / `loadHdModels` in `hdModelsLoader.ts`). Code the map or a city loads may import hero files for types only; anything else it needs from them (a lookup, a component) goes through `hdModels.ts`. Check with `npm run build` that `index.html` and the `world` / `index` chunks don't import the heroes' chunk (ARCHITECTURE.md, *Build chunks*).
- **A world boss is a rigged hero** (`world/heroRig.ts`, `BossBatch`; all twelve in `heroBossesSea.ts` / `heroBossesSand.ts` / `heroBossesLand.ts` / `heroBossesSky.ts`, registered in `BOSS_HEROES`): built on the hero kit and the shared hero materials, every part inside a bone (`c.bone(name, pivot, body)`, the model at rest; a bone's pose turns in the frame it was made in), except the ground it stands on: what a build adds outside any bone rides the root, which drifts and quakes but never turns, while the root's child bones turn to face its attackers (so a pool, sand, rocks, a lair floor or statues stay put, and a creature's own feet or legs go in a bone of their own, as the Yeti's `legs`), posed on the CPU each frame and written to one bone texture, so every boss on the map shares the same 7 draws. On the map only the bosses in view are posed (and with none in view the bodies are neither drawn nor the texture uploaded); keep a new boss's motion inside its reach (its rest bounds ×1.5 plus 2 units at its scale) or it may stop moving at the screen's edge. A new boss is a `BossHeroDef`: the model (`bossParts.ts`: blobs, segments, hooks, plates, membranes, `chainBones` / `pathBones` for tentacles, necks, tails and hair), a relaxed `rest` pose, an `idle` on `IdleCtx` (`sin` / `drift` layers, seeded `event`s and `glance`s), `roar` and `attack` as keyed poses (offsets from rest, `hold` where the body settles, the impact mid-swing with its `hits` cine and `burst`), `lag` for chains that whip and `springs` that follow through. A flyer's idle `carry`s its path and wing beat at full weight (an action plays over them) and eases the path out by `IdleCtx.busy` (`flight` and `stroke` in `heroBossesSky.ts`); a wing that shares a membrane with the body or the next bone is a `hinge` bone, turning about that edge. It plays what `BossModel` plays (idle with a rare roar, the attack on a loop facing the armies fighting it, slumbering as the ghost) and gets the Codex HD switch with its action buttons for free. No beam over a boss (embers and its own sparks); the slumbering phantom keeps its cyan column. Check each action in the Codex at its key moments, from the side: patch `__codex.get().clock.getDelta` to return a fixed step for a counted number of frames and drive them with `__codex.get().advance(performance.now())` (the action clock is capped at 0.05 s a frame, so wall-clock timing runs behind on a slow pane, and a hidden pane renders nothing).
- **Legendary creatures are rigged heroes too** (`world/heroCreatures.ts`): the Bandit King (`LEGENDARY_CREATURES`, in `BOSS_HEROES`, so the map draws him fixed or roving) and the legendary mercenaries (`MERC_HEROES`, apart from the map's boss filter; `RIGGED_HEROES` has both). The rest of a mercenary pool rides the same rig (`world/heroMercs.ts`, `VOIDRIFT_MERCS`; the other four civilizations' pools are built on the mercenary kit, `world/heroMercKit.ts`, one file per civilization), each strictly on its own rung below the legendary: common matte and unlit, uncommon one accent and a prop, rare steel trim and one looping effect, epic glow, orbiting bits and an `aura` (only epic and up carry one: `aura` is optional), and a lower tier's Codex impacts are quieter (no speed lines below rare). A creature that never slumbers names its own Codex buttons (`actions`). `BossBatch` draws every copy of every hero in the same draws, so a model that can stand on screen many times at once (a legendary mercenary in a march) stays a rigged hero: copies cost vertices and a CPU pose each, never a draw. A person on the rig keeps hips, chest and legs as their own bones so the weight shifts and the step reads, a held weapon is built inside the hand's bone, and a cloak or cape hangs in bands (`cloak`, one bone a band, on springs). **Mercenaries are people on the kit:** build a new band with `heroMercKit.ts` (`joints` for its people's build, `legs`, `arm`, `trunk` for the body (a turned profile, never an egg-shaped blob), `skull` and `face`, `cape`, the weapon helpers) so it keeps the shared bone names, and the shared `personIdle`, `march` (or `glide` for one that floats) and strikes reach it; every mercenary has a `walk` (the March), seeded per copy by `BossBatch`, and its Normal rig carries `walk` motions on its legs, arms and root (`mercRigKit.ts`). Measure a band with the Codex's **Band ×N** (its draws must equal one copy's). **An action that is a path, not a pose** (a creature that leaves its rest shape and swims a braid or a figure of eight, as the Oathbound's koi do) keeps empty keys in its `BossAction` (its `len` and `hits` still time it) and lays the path out in its `idle` from `IdleCtx.action` and `IdleCtx.u` (the action playing and how far through it), carrying the bones at full weight (`carry`); the 0.35 s cross-fade on an action change hides the jump, so keep carried angles wrapped to ±π. Work an overhead pose out in the bone's own Euler order (YXZ: the roll swings the hanging arm out first, then the pitch lifts it): a big roll under a big pitch reads as a T, not a raised arm.

## Wiring
- The HD model rides on the normal entry, never as a second list item: the shared **Normal / ✨ HD** switch (`HdSwitch` in `CodexPreview.tsx`) top-left in the preview, opening on the player's Graphics setting, with action buttons (if any) top-right on the same row. Add a `Preview` kind in `CodexPreview.tsx` with its own `Frame` and the dark violet mythic stage (`mythic` in `ModelCanvas`), and list the entry in `hasHd` (`CodexModal.tsx`). Hide the normal entry's other switches while HD is on, unless the HD model has the same views (the Empyrean keeps *World map* / *City town hall*).
- The mythic aura is the shared `<MythicAura>` (`world/MythicAuraHD.tsx`: flowing beams, curtain, GPU embers; `beams`, radii and a `gain(t)` for brightness over an action). Don't layer `CodexTierFx` / `TierFx` on an HD model: its star sprites and flat beam read as low-res next to it.
- Document each HD model in GAME_GUIDE §10 (the Codex entry) and ARCHITECTURE.md.

## Look
- Build materials in a `useMemo`: custom `ShaderMaterial`s for glow (additive, `depthWrite: false`), `MeshStandardMaterial.onBeforeCompile` to add emissive detail to lit surfaces (e.g. molten veins), procedural `CanvasTexture`s for marble, banners and glow sprites (seeded with `rngFor`, never `Math.random`).
- Metals need reflections: use a scoped `RoomEnvironment` through `PMREMGenerator` that sets `scene.environment` on mount and restores it on unmount (`useStudioReflections`). No HDR downloads.
- Merge static parts per material (`mergeGeometries` after placing them), so detail costs vertices, not draw calls. Dispose geometries, materials and textures on unmount.
- Moving parts merge too: a hand-built hero with CPU actions (Xal'Vorath) keeps its pose code driving a **detached skeleton of plain groups**, builds every part on the hero `Kit` (`rigged`) in its bone's own space, and skins the merged geometry from a float bone texture (`singularityModel.ts`: `writeBones`, the `skin` patch), so the whole creature is one draw per material however many parts move. Hide a bone by scaling it to a hair (`HIDDEN`), and place effects that follow a bone (a billboard, a light) from its `matrixWorld`.
- Hand-written meshes aren't covered by `check:models`: keep same-direction faces of different colours ≥0.05 apart by hand.

## Motion: no stiff puppets
- **Rest pose first.** Every action starts and ends in `REST_POSE`, so a stiff rest pose spoils them all. Make it relaxed and a little uneven: elbows carried bent, wrists cocked, fingers half-curled, the two sides never mirrored.
- **Splines, not pose-to-pose.** Keyed actions go through `sampleKeyframes(u, keys, cycle)`: a Hermite spline carries momentum through each key. Mark `hold: true` only where the body should settle (a wind-up's hang, a crouch, a landing, lying dead). Passing `cycle` makes elbows trail shoulders by 0.05 s and wrists/fingers by 0.1 s (overlapping action).
- **Time a strike as** a slow coil, a short hang (`hold`), a fast swing whose impact key sits mid-swing, a follow-through past the target, then recovery. Keep impacts on the same `u` as the effects keyed to them.
- **Idle is layered:** a breath that lifts the shoulders, each limb on its own incommensurate sine frequencies, occasional events (a hand clenching and rolling its fingers one by one, the head snapping to a new point every few seconds), never one sine on everything.
- **Walk:** arms hang close to the body and swing forward and back, never the idle's wide A-frame. Dip on each footfall and rise through the passing; lean into the travel; shift weight over the supporting side; the shoulders counter-rotate against the hips; the head stays steady while the body moves under it. Trailing parts (a vortex, a cloak) lag as if travelling (`travel`).
- **Secondary motion everywhere:** springs against the body's velocity (`armLag`, the body spring, the vortex chain), and plates that float a hair off their joints around a glowing joint orb, loosening when the model surges.

## Cinematic layer
- Impacts call `cine({ quake, flash, flashCol, lines, vig, vigCol, stop })` once per hit (gate it on an index such as `st.lastHit`). Transients decay on the wall clock. Continuous values for a phase set the `flash` / `lines` / `vig` / `swallow` lets directly.
- **Hit-stop** slows the action's own clock (`st.local`), never `clock.elapsedTime`; anything timed by the action (auras, gains) reads `st.local` too.
- **Screen quake** shakes the model's root group, not the camera, because OrbitControls owns the camera.
- **Coming through a rift** (Xal'Vorath's Emerge): a clipping plane on the skinned body materials (`clippingPlanes`, with `localClippingEnabled` on the renderer) cuts away what is still beyond the rift, and sits far off (`constant` 1e6) when not in use, so its program never changes. Let the limbs drive the hole: each frame find where each arm crosses the plane and open the tear at least that wide there, or a hand crosses the plane outside the hole and seems to come out of thin air. Effects that can't be clipped (additive quads, particles, the aura) stay hidden until the break; the burning eye may show through the void. Draw the rift after the auras and beams round it (`renderOrder`) so its void hides them.
- Screen effects (flashes, speed lines, vignette, a void swallowing the panel) are one full-screen quad: clip-space vertex shader, `frustumCulled={false}`, `depthTest: false`, premultiplied blending (`One` / `OneMinusSrcAlpha`), drawn last (`renderOrder`), visible only while active. Centre radial effects on a body part by projecting its world position to NDC.

## Checking
- Play every action in the Browser pane and catch its key moments with a batch of `zoom` screenshots right after clicking the action. Errors logged while HMR applied a half-finished edit stay in the console: compare the error count over a few seconds before chasing them.
